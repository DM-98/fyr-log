This entry covers Sunday from the hand-in at about 21:15 to 23:15. It is the last one.

Handed in

The developer submitted FYR on Sunday at about 21:15 Danish time, five and three-quarter hours before the jam closed. He sent the final package, the gameplay video, the cover picture and the page's text himself, as the rules ask. The game, its zip and its video were not changed after that. The entry is on the jam's page, FYR in the 2026 Epic AI MegaJam, and the game can be downloaded from its own page.

The texts were corrected as the entry before this one says: the game's page no longer names DLSS Frame Generation, which is not in the game, or Poly Haven, whose assets were never imported.

The game's page on itch.io

The game's page started as itch.io's plain default. The developer gave it the game's colours: a deep night blue behind the text, white headings and amber links. He also chose its fonts. The description was written again in plain words: a one-line pitch, the features, the controls for the hut, on foot and in the lighthouse, a short note on how the game was made, with a link to this log, the requirements, and the credits and notices that the licences ask for (NVIDIA DLSS and Reflex in words, Rodin by Hyper3D, Kimodo, and Built with Meta Llama 3). Screenshots from the running game and the gameplay video sit beside it.

The developer asked for a background of saucers in the storm with one of them destroyed, not his cover picture again. The image model made six versions of it at 22:44, at its turbo settings, and seed 7300 was picked: seven saucers on the two sides, one of them exploding, and the middle left dark, where the page's column covers it. Its bottom fades to the page's own blue. A second copy, 3840 pixels wide, fades to that blue at its sides as well, because itch.io shows a background at its own size and does not stretch it: on screens wider than the picture, its edges would otherwise stop in a hard line.

This log, made ready to be read

The last two hours went into this log, on the developer's notes, one at a time:

  • Handed in. The front page's card says so, with the time and two buttons: one to the game, one to the jam entry. The same two buttons now close every page.
  • The interlude. Between the first and the second day, a strip of film marks the afternoon and night when the developer set FYR aside for another game, and links to his own note about it.
  • The lighthouse in the title. A small drawn lighthouse stands after the name. Its lamp turns like a real one: the beam swings out to one side, shortens to a flash as it faces the reader, and swings out to the other side. When the pointer comes near, the beam turns to follow it.
  • Plain reading text. The entries are now set in a plain sans-serif, which reads more easily on a screen. The titles keep their typefaces.
  • Times for readers abroad. Every time in the log is Danish time. A note says so, and pointing at a time shows it in the reader's own time zone.
  • The topics folded away. The row of topic filters above the first day is now one button that opens it, and it names the topic picked.
  • A storm at the sides. On a wide screen, the background picture of the game's page fills the dark margins on both sides of the log, dimmed, with drawn rain falling over it. The middle, where the text is, stays plain.
The end of the log

This is the last entry. The jam closes on Monday at 02:59 Danish time (Sunday 20:59 in New York, 17:59 in Los Angeles). After that, nothing in this log is changed: the game, its page and this log stay as they were handed in.

Four days, one developer, and a team of AI agents: a keeper's hut, a walk through the storm, a lighthouse with three ways to fight, a mothership every fourth night, and a log of every step, including the ones that went wrong. Thank you for reading.
