Day one, cleared on foot

At 03:32 the verifier started day one with the walking automaton, on the build of 03:29. It read the letter and took the lighter at 0:13, had the first thing on the truck at 0:33, brought wood from the hut at 1:56, lit the fire at 2:50, took the dead man's camera at 3:08 and burned the first thing at 3:33. "Places." came at 5:20, and at 5:27 the buyer said: "That is the hall and the dining room done. Bed." No stall, no death, and with the graphics card to itself the game ran at 154 to 169 frames a second, with no frame longer than 24 milliseconds.

Failure An hour earlier the same test got nowhere

In the run at 02:15 the automaton found no free floor beside the hall table or the clock, lifted the clock from too far off, and stood 1.6 metres up at the foot of the great stair, holding it, until the run ended. The Director, meanwhile, stuck on the cellar stair thirteen times. Both stair faults and the hall table's were gone by the build of 03:29.

The verifier's verdict on this run, retold: the first ten minutes now hold the whole design. The letter, the lighter, the work, the forest, the fire catching, his camera and the voice that says "That stays.", the dusk, "Places.", and the floor done. On foot the automaton finished at 5:27; a first-time player is slower, and will meet the dark and "Places." with one or two things still to carry. That is the tension the design asks for. A run that goes on to bed and the day's tally is waiting for its turn.

The ground comes back

The ground's surface lacked one setting that the engine's newest geometry needs, so the engine drew it with a plain white default. With the setting on, the earth and the grass came back, and the dead man's film cable now runs along the forest path from the yard to the hut, with a branch to each of his lamps. It is in the target picture, and it shows the way.

The Director's face

The second figure of the Director, which came into the game white at midnight, has had its surfaces since about one. Its head is a separate bust grafted onto the body under the scarf: a face with black sockets, a hanging jaw and liver spots.

Failure By a strong light he still turns white

In the flashlight's beam at one to three metres the second figure's face and jacket still bleach to white, like plaster. By day and by a flame he is right. Capping how bright he may get did not help; the agent found that the cause is the exposure (the wall four metres behind him is exposed right, and he, at one metre, gets sixteen times its light), and is now measuring the beam on him and darkening him by it. Not yet seen in a picture.

Where the night's hours went

Failure Three minutes of compiling at every start

Since about 02:40 every start of the game spends about three minutes compiling shaders before the first frame. The test scripts give a game two minutes to show progress, so they killed the first two runs of the verifier as frozen. The verifier gave its runs longer; the cause, a cache that is not kept between runs, is not fixed. The developer's own first start may show a long black screen.

Failure A lock held for forty minutes

Eight agents share one build and one game run at a time, through a lock. At 03:44 one run moved its log to another place, and the script that frees the lock waits for a line in that log. So the lock stayed taken for forty minutes while a build, a package and four other runs waited. It was the second time in the night: the same mistake had held the lock at three o'clock. Two agents found the cause independently and wrote it on the main board, with the cure for the script.

Failure Fixes that no picture has seen

The house agent took the grey dust off the dark wood and the floors at 02:28 and again at 03:41, and could not get the project for a run to see it. The light agent did the opposite with the night's exposure: two newer settings that had been built but never seen in a picture were taken out again, and the last one judged from pictures was put back. Nothing unseen goes into the morning's build.

Next
